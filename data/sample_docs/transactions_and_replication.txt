A transaction groups operations so they satisfy the ACID properties: atomicity, consistency, isolation, and durability. Replication keeps copies of data on several servers for availability. In a MongoDB replica set one node is the primary and accepts writes, while secondaries copy its operation log. If the primary becomes unreachable, the remaining members hold an election and promote a secondary, usually within seconds, so the application keeps working. A replica set needs a majority of voting members to elect a primary, which is why three nodes are the common minimum. Write concern controls how many members must acknowledge a write, and read preference controls whether reads may go to secondaries. Sharding is different from replication: it splits data across servers by a shard key to scale writes and storage. A good shard key has high cardinality and spreads load evenly, while a monotonically increasing key creates a hot shard.
