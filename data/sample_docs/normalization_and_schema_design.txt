Normalization organizes relational tables to reduce redundancy and update anomalies. First normal form requires atomic column values. Second normal form removes partial dependencies on part of a composite key. Third normal form removes transitive dependencies, so non-key attributes depend only on the key. Document databases such as MongoDB take a different approach: you model data around how the application reads it. Embed data that is small, read together, and owned by the parent, such as tags or an address. Reference data that is large, shared, or grows without bound, such as chunks of a document or comments on a post. Embedding gives one read and atomic single-document updates, while referencing avoids oversized documents, since a single MongoDB document is limited to 16 megabytes. The right choice depends on the read and write patterns, not on a fixed rule.
